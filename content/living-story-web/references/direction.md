# Direction and choreography

## Derive the world

Find one meaningful relationship in the project: connection, transformation, discovery, precision, movement, growth, or craft. Translate that relationship into a world rather than translating every noun into a decoration.

For example, an archive may reveal nested histories; a manufacturing service may show material becoming a product; a music project may turn rhythm into changing space. These are prompts for reasoning, not reusable default themes.

Select a protagonist only if useful: a person, mascot, product, machine, object, ensemble, or environment. Preserve supplied identities and brand constraints. Do not force a character into a brand that calls for an object-led experience.

## Compose for attention

Choose one focal action at a time. Stage wide context, medium action, and close detail with readable pauses. Keep captions away from faces, hands, and action-critical objects. Use typography as composition without converting essential text into inaccessible artwork.

Use a few deliberate transitions:
- Match cut: preserve shape or direction across a change of scene.
- Panel crossing: allow a subject to cross an illustrated border with correct occlusion.
- Scale journey: move from a small detail into a larger environment with a recognizable anchor.
- Unfolding: reveal depth as a flat surface opens.
- Convergence: combine multiple explanatory panels into one resolved composition.

Explain the transition's purpose in the storyboard. Reject spectacle that obscures the product or adds empty scrolling.

## Motion phrasing

Use anticipation, primary action, follow-through, and a readable hold. Let attention lead body movement. Ground walking in foot contact and weight transfer. Give secondary motion a smaller amplitude and different timing than the primary action.

Do not use persistent floating to imply locomotion. Do not use unrelated pose crossfades to imply articulation. An intentional stepped comic style can be appropriate when requested, but do not present it as smooth continuous animation.

## Adapt the narrative surface

Keep navigation, product understanding, and primary actions available. Do not introduce a story gate into operational dashboards. On mobile, redesign framing and simplify secondary scenery before removing meaningful action. Use a static composition for reduced motion that conveys the same information.
