# Team Management

## Overview

Team management is the discipline of getting results through people you lead directly: running 1:1s that surface real problems early, giving feedback with the SBI (Situation–Behavior–Impact) model, delegating with the 70% rule, and conducting performance reviews that calibrate rather than surprise. This skill covers the four core practices — 1:1s, feedback, delegation, performance reviews — with scripts, cadences, and failure modes for each.

The core principle: **management is a practice, not a personality trait.** Each skill below is a repeatable routine with a template; consistency beats intensity, and the most common failure is skipping the routine because the week got busy.

## The Four Core Practices

| Practice | Cadence | Purpose | Failure Mode Without It |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1:1s** | Weekly, 30 min | Build trust, surface blockers early, coach career | Problems arrive at review time as surprises |
| **Feedback** | Within 48h of event | Correct course while context is fresh | Resentment builds; correction lands as blame |
| **Delegation** | Per task | Grow capability, free senior time | Manager becomes bottleneck; ICs stall at current level |
| **Performance reviews** | Quarterly formal | Calibrate expectations, align on growth | Reviews feel arbitrary; ratings shock people |

## 1:1s (Weekly, 30 Minutes)

The 1:1 is the report's meeting, not yours. Structure:

```text
[5 min]  Their agenda first — ask "what's on your mind?"
[10 min] Follow-ups — progress since last week, blockers removed?
[10 min] Coaching — one growth topic, one current challenge
[5 min]  Recap — decisions made, who does what by when
```

**Rules:**
- Never cancel; reschedule within the same week. Cancellation signals the meeting (and them) is low priority.
- Take shared notes; decisions and action items are written, not remembered.
- Ask "what would you change about how we work together?" at least monthly — the answer is the pulse of the relationship.

## Feedback: The SBI Model

Deliver all corrective and positive feedback as Situation–Behavior–Impact, within 48 hours of the event:

| Component | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Situation** | When and where, specifically | "In yesterday's design review…" |
| **Behavior** | What they observably did — no interpretation | "…you interrupted Priya twice while she was presenting the benchmark data…" |
| **Impact** | The concrete effect on work, team, or outcomes | "…which cut her section short and she didn't get to the cost analysis; the room couldn't evaluate it." |

**Rules:**
- Describe behavior, never character ("you interrupted" not "you're rude").
- No sandwich — name the topic, deliver SBI, ask for their view, agree next step.
- Positive feedback uses the same SBI structure; vague praise ("great job") teaches nothing.
- In private for corrective; in public for positive (when the person enjoys visibility).

## Delegation: The 70% Rule

Delegate any task where the delegate can do it **70% as well as you** — not 100%. The remaining 30% gap is where their growth happens, and waiting for a 100% match means never delegating anything that stretches people.

```text
  Your quality ──────────────────────── 100%
  Delegate now if ─────────── 70% ◄───┐
  Their current ──────────── 70–90%   │ growth zone
                                      ┘
```

**Delegation protocol:**
1. **Define outcome, not method** — state what "done" looks like and constraints (deadline, budget, quality bar); let them choose the path.
2. **Agree check-in points** — milestone reviews, not continuous monitoring. Micromanaging deletes the growth value.
3. **Set the failure budget** — name what a safe mistake looks like and what must never happen.
4. **Debrief** — after completion, ask what they'd do differently before adding your own notes.

**Never delegate:** performance feedback about their teammates, personnel decisions, or conversations you should be having yourself.

## Performance Reviews (Quarterly)

A review is a calibration of the period, not a verdict on the person. No rating should ever be a surprise — if it is, the 1:1s and feedback failed first.

**Structure:**
1. **Self-review first** — have them assess against the same criteria before you share yours; the delta is the conversation.
2. **Evidence, not vibes** — every rating cites 2–3 concrete events (SBI records from the quarter). "Strong on delivery" means nothing; "shipped the payments migration a week early with zero rollback" is a rating.
3. **Growth plan** — one strength to leverage further, one growth area with a specific next assignment or practice, reviewed at the next cycle.
4. **Calibrate across your team** — before delivering, compare ratings across reports to counter leniency/hardass drift; in larger orgs, join the manager calibration session.

**Rating hygiene:** separate performance (results this period) from potential (trajectory); never let a promotion timeline leak into a performance rating.

## Anti-Rationalization Table

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I'll figure it out as I go" | Management habits form from repetition — improvised 1:1s drift into status meetings and stay there. Follow the templates. |
| "They're doing fine, no feedback needed" | Silence reads as indifference, and uncorrected small behaviors compound into review-time surprises. Feedback within 48h, positive included. |
| "It's faster to just do it myself" | True once, expensive forever. The 70% rule exists precisely because the 30% gap never closes without delegation. |
| "The review will be awkward, I'll soften it" | Softened ratings mislead the person and corrupt calibration for everyone who rated honestly. Evidence-based ratings, delivered kindly. |
| "I'll cancel this week's 1:1, busy week" | The busy weeks are when the meeting matters most — cancellation exactly when pressure is high teaches people to hide problems. |

## Process

1. **Instrument the cadence** — calendar-block weekly 1:1s; never cancel, only reschedule in-week.
2. **Practice SBI** — within 48h of any notable event, deliver one SBI (positive or corrective).
3. **Delegate one 70% task** — pick something a report can do 70% as well; hand over outcome + constraints + check-ins.
4. **Run quarterly reviews by evidence** — self-review first, SBI citations per rating, calibration pass before delivery.
5. **Review your own practice** — monthly: which routine slipped, why, and what prevents the slip next month.
